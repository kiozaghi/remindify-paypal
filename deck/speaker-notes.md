# Remindify — speaker notes

15 slides · ~8 minutes at a calm pace, ~12 minutes if you take questions inline.
Every number here is checkable. If you are asked something not in the deck, say "I don't know yet" and write it down.

---

## 1 — Cover (20s)

> "Everyone in this room has a second job they never applied for. It doesn't have a title or a salary, and nobody can see it. Remindify is the register for that job."

Don't explain the product yet. Land the sentence, then move.

## 2 — The problem (60s)

Two halves, and say them as two halves:

- **People:** the refill before Friday, the school form, the friend's visa deadline.
- **Money:** rent, payroll, insurance renewal, the filing that expires quietly.

> "The family group chat is the system of record. That is the actual competitor."

Close with the pull quote: *every one of them comes back; none of them has an owner.*

## 3 — Why nothing fixes it (50s)

Calendars know the date. Task apps know the work. Accountants own one lane. Marketplaces match a person to a job once.

> "Each tool holds a fragment. The thing that actually fails is the handover — and no tool has a receipt for a handover."

The two cards are the thesis: delegation without proof, and agents without accountability.

## 4 — The object (70s)

This is the most important slide. An obligation is not a reminder; it is a small data structure built to survive being delegated.

Read the card out loud, field by field: owner, backup, consequence, autonomy ceiling, proof, chain. Point at **"Agent may prepare the renewal, not pay it."**

> "Autonomy is a ceiling, not a switch. That one line is why a person can safely hand this over."

## 5 — Two ledgers (50s)

> "The first screen counts both sides before it asks for an email. 'How much are you carrying?' That question is the onboarding."

Then packs: a pack is a year somebody already mapped — with the proof requirement attached. Mention the tax-filing pack is the shape of the first paid wedge.

## 6 — Remind, then do (60s)

Three paths, one standard: you do it, your agent does it, or the bench does it.

> "Reminding is the commodity. Completion is the product."

Land the ladder (notify → prepare → approve → autopilot) and the line: *a reminder tells you; Remindify finishes it — or proves why it didn't.*

## 7 — Agents as citizens (60s)

Passports, earned autonomy, human/agent parity, machine access, containment.

> "An agent moves up by filing accepted proof, not by asking for permission. And it can never act outside the ceiling the owner set."

If asked what is live: agent passports, scopes, ceilings and audit exist; the MCP/REST surface exists. Do not imply a public agent SDK — that is roadmap.

## 8 — Trust layer (70s)

Walk the four steps, then the transactions.

- Anchor → approve → fund → release, all recorded on **Base Sepolia** on 29 October 2026.
- Contracts are **source-verified** and **unaudited**.
- `anchored`, `funded`, `released` were read back from the contracts as true.

> "No token, no custody, no documents onchain. The chain stores a hash that a buyer can check without trusting our database."

Say "testnet" before anyone asks. It buys credibility; hiding it costs it.

## 9 — Traction (60s)

Read the strip honestly. Nine surfaces, 35 tests, four onchain transactions, two verified contracts — and **zero paying users**.

> "I am pre-revenue on purpose. The next milestone is ten design partners on Base, not a growth number I can't defend."

The right-hand column is deliberate: not mainnet, not audited, no Celo yet, repo private. Say it before diligence does.

## 10 — Go to market (50s)

Start where obligation density is highest: founders, freelancers and small studios in Addis and the diaspora.

> "They already pay for fragments — an accountant, a broker, a VA — and still miss things. Their counterparties are abroad, so 'the proof is in my email' doesn't work."

Ladder: Addis + diaspora → English-speaking solo operators → platforms that embed the layer.

## 11 — Business model (50s)

Personal $0, Studio $18/mo, Operator $48/mo, 15% marketplace take, usage-based agent platform.

> "We price against the penalty, not against a to-do list. One lapsed policy is $1,240; the seat that prevents it is $18."

Be ready to explain the 15%: provider is paid only when proof is accepted, so the take is on completed work.

## 12 — Why Base, why now (50s)

Three reasons: anchoring is cheap enough for small frequent receipts; USDC is already how these users move dollars; Base explicitly funds agent infrastructure.

Then the discipline: mainnet after a contract review, Celo reuses the same EVM module, **Ethiopia stays offchain until the NBE authorises virtual-asset activity**.

## 13 — Roadmap (40s)

Now → 90 days → 12 months → 18 months. Emphasise the 90-day block: mainnet + review, ten design partners, mobile capture, first paid Studio workspaces.

Targets at the bottom: 10,000 activated users, 1,000 jobs at 95% proof acceptance, three platform design partners.

> "The bet: proof-of-done becomes the default way small teams delegate."

## 14 — The ask (50s)

$400k for 10%, 18-month runway. Allocation: 50% engineering, 25% design and product, 15% marketplace supply, 10% GTM.

> "Enough to get the trust layer to mainnet, ten design partners live, and the first thousand marketplace jobs proven."

Point at the diligence strip: everything in the deck is checkable at the URLs on screen.

## 15 — Team and close (30s)

One founder, end to end: interface, API, agents, contracts, deployment. CTO and Master Trainer at Exopya; 600+ designs, 8 brand builds.

> "The pain is lived, not researched. This is the register I needed for my own life — and it is already running."

Close on the line: *everyone has a second job they never applied for; we are building the register that gives it back.*

## Q&A prep

**"Why won't Notion/Google/Apple just do this?"**
They hold documents and dates. They do not hold consequence, backup ownership, autonomy ceilings or proof. The register is a different object, and the proof layer is the defensible part.

**"Why would anyone pay $18?"**
Because the alternative is a $1,240 lapse, a fine, or a bounced payroll. We are priced against the penalty.

**"Why crypto at all?"**
Because the buyer and the provider often don't trust the same database, and the provider wants payment on delivery. A hash and a USDC release solve both without a token.

**"What's the moat?"**
The receipt graph: who completed what, against which proof standard, accepted by whom. It compounds per workspace and per pack, and it is portable for the agent.

**"Why you?"**
I have shipped the whole thing alone, and I have lived the problem. The next hire is engineering, and I know exactly which surface to hand over first.

**"What would make you stop?"**
If ten design partners use it and still miss obligations, the object is wrong — not the marketing. I would rebuild the object before raising again.
