The product

One object, built to be handed over.

An obligation is not a reminder. It carries everything needed to survive being delegated: who owns it, who catches it, what happens if it slips, how far an agent may go, and what counts as done.

  • Risk is severity × urgency × confidence × autonomy — with the money exposure and the plain-language consequence.
  • Autonomy is a ceiling, not a switch. An agent earns each step.
  • Proof is required by the item, not by the mood. No proof, no completion.
  • Everything is attributed: reads, writes, approvals, runs, receipts, exports.

Remind, then do

Reminding is the commodity.
Completion is the product.

Path one

You do it

Open the item, see the consequence and the proof it needs, and close it with evidence. Thirty seconds, not thirty tabs.

e.g. sign the school form → proof: signed copy
Path two

Your agent does it

Agents get passports: scopes, ceilings, credentials. They act inside the ceiling you set, then file proof — not a status update.

e.g. draft the renewal, stop for approval → proof: run log + receipt
Path three

The bench does it

Hire a human or a community agent for one obligation. Fixed price, scoped access, USDC released when the proof is accepted.

e.g. file the return, fixed price → proof: accepted delivery, escrow releases

The 2026 difference

Agents are first-class
citizens, not features.

  • Passports. Scopes, trust score, success rate, autonomy ceiling, machine-readable export.
  • Earned autonomy. An agent moves up by filing accepted proof — not by asking for permission.
  • Human/agent parity. A chartered accountant and a tax-filing agent sit on the same bench, under the same proof standard.
  • Machine access. REST and MCP, scoped keys, metering, idempotency, signed webhooks.
  • Containment. No agent can act outside its ceiling, and every call is attributed in the audit trail.

Trust layer

Proof, not promises.
The chain checks the work.

01

Work happens

You, your agent or a provider completes the obligation and attaches the artifact.

02

Receipt is signed

Proof and evidence are hashed with keccak-256, then signed as EIP-712 typed data.

03

Anchor on Base

anchored[receiptId] = true — verifiable without trusting our database.

04

Payment releases

USDC escrow pays the provider only against that anchored receipt. No proof, no money.
